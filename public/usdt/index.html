01Part 01 · Intro

USDT, explained

How can a coin always be worth exactly one dollar?

No finance knowledge needed. We start with Lan's savings.

Lan is a freelance designer in Da Nang, paid by a client abroad. She wants to keep part of her money in US dollars — but a dollar account is slow and complicated to open. A friend says: "Just hold USDT." Scroll down.

USDT is a receipt for a dollar in a vault.

A company called Tether holds dollars (and things almost as good as dollars). For every dollar it holds, it creates one USDT — a token that lives on a blockchain and can be sent anywhere in minutes.

That is why its price stays at about $1: in principle, one USDT can always be handed back for one dollar.

Worth knowing

About 184 billion USDT exist — a sum equal to more than a third of Vietnam's whole economy in 2025 (GDP ≈ $514 billion).

So the whole story comes down to one question: is the money really in the vault?

First we open the vault and see how it works. Then twelve years of history — including the years when nobody could check — and finally USDT in Vietnam, its strengths, its weaknesses and a conclusion.

In short

USDT is a digital dollar issued by a private company, Tether. It is worth $1 as long as people trust that Tether really holds a dollar for every token.

Two receipts: a customer deposits 1,000,000 USD and Tether prints 1,000,000 USDT; another returns 1,000,000 USDT, Tether burns them and pays the dollars back. A rubber band keeps the price at $1; a counter shows about 183.5 billion USDT.

02Part 02 · How the vault works

1 · Dollars in, tokens out

QWhere do new USDT come from — and where do they go?

Deposit a dollar, a token is printed.

A big customer — say a trading company — sends Tether 1 million dollars by bank transfer. Tether creates ("prints") 1 million new USDT and sends them to the customer's wallet.

Nothing magical: the dollars go into Tether's reserve, the tokens go out into the world.

Hand the token back, it is burned.

When a customer returns 1 million USDT and asks for dollars, Tether pays the dollars and destroys ("burns") the tokens. Only verified customers, mostly companies, can do this directly with Tether.

So the number of USDT should always match the dollars Tether owes.

Worth knowing

Tether earns almost nothing from people sending USDT to each other. Its money comes from the dollars you hand it — which is the subject of chapter 3.

Lan never meets Tether. So why is her USDT still worth $1?

Ordinary people buy USDT from other people or on an exchange. Big traders keep that price near $1: if USDT drops to $0.99, they buy the cheap tokens and hand them to Tether for a full dollar; if it rises to $1.01, they get new tokens for $1 and sell them.

That simple trade works like a rubber band, pulling the price back to one dollar.

About 183.5 billion USDT exist today.

At the end of September 2026, Tether reported about 183.5 billion USDT in issue — the third-largest crypto asset after Bitcoin and Ethereum.

Each one is a promise of one dollar. Next: what exactly backs those promises?

In short

New USDT are printed when dollars come in and burned when dollars go out. Traders keep the price near $1 by buying cheap tokens and cashing them in, or printing new ones and selling them.

03Part 02 · How the vault works

2 · Open the vault: a company that lends more to America than Germany does

QWhat backs 183.5 billion promises?

191.5 billion dollars of assets.

Every three months Tether publishes a reserve report checked by an accounting firm (BDO). On 30 September 2026 it listed $191.5 billion of assets against $183.5 billion owed to USDT holders.

Let's turn that into 100 squares, each worth about $1.9 billion.

69 squares: lending to the US government.

Most of the vault is US Treasury bills — short IOUs from the US government, repaid within about three months — plus overnight loans backed by them. Together, about $132 billion.

These are among the safest and easiest assets to sell in finance, and they make Tether one of the biggest holders of US government debt.

Worth knowing

If Tether were a country, it would be about the 17th-largest holder of US government debt — ahead of Germany, South Korea and Saudi Arabia.

10 squares of gold, 4 of bitcoin.

Tether also keeps about $19.1 billion of physical gold and $8.4 billion of bitcoin. Their prices move — a lot, in bitcoin's case.

When they rise, the vault grows. When they fall, the cushion shrinks.

Worth knowing

Tether holds roughly 140 tonnes of gold in a Swiss vault — more than the national reserves of Australia, Qatar, South Korea or Greece. In 2025 only Poland's central bank bought more gold.

17 squares: "other".

The rest — about $31.6 billion — is a mix of cash-like funds, secured loans and other investments. Tether publishes less detail about these, and that is exactly what critics worry about.

96 squares are owed; 4 are the cushion. Now shake the vault.

About $183.5 billion belongs, in effect, to USDT holders. The difference — about $7.95 billion — is Tether's cushion ("excess reserves"). It nearly doubled in the third quarter of 2026, mostly because bitcoin and gold rose.

A cushion of about 4% is thin: one analyst calculated that a fall of about 15% in Tether's non-cash assets would wipe it out.

Tether reserve report, 30 Sep 2026 (BDO). A report like this checks one day's figures; it is not a full audit.

Worth knowing

Stress test: if gold, bitcoin and loans lost about 15%, the four cushion squares would be gone — and USDT would no longer be fully backed on paper.

In short

About seven tenths of the vault is short-term US government debt. The rest includes gold, bitcoin and loans — assets whose value can move — above a thin cushion of about 4%.

Lan's 1,000 USDT earn 0%; the dollars behind them, invested in Treasury bills, earn interest for Tether. Bars show Tether's reported profit: $6.2 billion (2023), $13 billion (2024), $10.1 billion (2025), $4.5 billion (January–September 2026).

04Part 02 · How the vault works

3 · The most profitable vault in the world

QIf Lan's USDT earns nothing, who earns the interest?

Lan earns 0%. Tether earns the interest.

Holding USDT pays no interest. But the dollars behind it sit in Treasury bills that do pay interest — several percent a year while US rates are high.

So the bigger USDT grows, the more Tether earns, without paying holders anything.

Billions a year.

Tether reported a profit of $6.2 billion in 2023, more than $13 billion in 2024 and $10.1 billion in 2025 — numbers close to the biggest Wall Street banks.

From January to September 2026 it reported $4.5 billion.

One employee, the profit of hundreds of bankers.

In 2024 Tether made about $13 billion with roughly 150 employees. Tether keeps profits as its own capital and invests much of it — in bitcoin, gold and companies in mining, AI and media; that portfolio passed $20 billion by the end of 2025.

Worth knowing

≈ $85 million of profit per employee in 2024 — about 300 times Goldman Sachs. One Tether employee earns what a whole floor of bankers earns.

Tether is a bet on US interest rates.

Tether's business fits in one line: borrow at 0% from USDT holders, lend to the US government at market rates. When rates fall, profit falls: 2025 profit was about 23% lower than 2024.

It works as long as holders keep their trust. If too many want dollars back at once, Tether must sell quickly — easy for Treasury bills, harder for loans or gold.

Worth knowing

Simple maths: on about $132 billion of Treasury bills, every 1 percentage point cut in US rates takes roughly $1.3 billion a year out of Tether's income.

In short

USDT holders get no interest; Tether keeps it. That makes Tether extremely profitable — and part of the money goes into riskier investments.

05Part 03 · Twelve years

4 · 2014: a dollar for crypto traders

QWhy would anyone need a dollar on a blockchain?

Realcoin, July 2014.

Three entrepreneurs — Brock Pierce, Reeve Collins and Craig Sellars — announced "Realcoin", a dollar-backed token running on top of the Bitcoin network. The first tokens were created on 6 October 2014; on 20 November it was renamed Tether.

The problem: banks are slow, crypto never sleeps.

Crypto exchanges trade 24 hours a day, but moving dollars between them meant bank transfers that took days — and many banks refused to work with crypto companies at all.

With USDT, a trader could hold "dollars" on an exchange and move them to another one in minutes.

The sister company: Bitfinex.

From the start Tether was tied to Bitfinex, a large crypto exchange, with the same owners and managers behind both. That link would cause most of the trouble later.

Paolo Ardoino, Bitfinex's technology chief, became Tether's CEO at the end of 2023. Giancarlo Devasini, Bitfinex's finance chief, controls the company.

Worth knowing

The world's biggest digital dollar was not created by a bank, but by the people running a crypto exchange — and it is still controlled by them.

Small for years, then everywhere.

At the start of 2017 there were only about 10 million USDT. Then traders discovered it: by mid-2021 there were over 60 billion, and USDT had become the main "dollar" of the crypto world.

In short

USDT was built for crypto traders who needed a fast dollar. From day one it was tied to the exchange Bitfinex — a link that later raised serious questions.

A vault with a "100% backed" sign; investigators find $850 million missing at Bitfinex's payment processor, covered with Tether's money; a calendar of 26 months where only 27.6% of days were fully backed; fines of $18.5 million and $41 million; in 2026, a KPMG audit stamp.

06Part 03 · Twelve years

5 · Is the money really there?

QWhat did regulators find when they finally looked?

"Fully backed", said the website.

For years Tether said every USDT was backed by one dollar in its bank accounts, and promised a proper audit. In 2017 it hired an accounting firm, but the relationship ended before any audit was finished.

2019: $850 million goes missing.

New York's Attorney General said Bitfinex had lost about $850 million handed to a payment company, Crypto Capital, and had covered the hole with money from Tether's reserve.

Bitfinex said the money had been seized by authorities in several countries rather than lost, and that it was trying to recover it.

2021: two settlements.

In February 2021 Tether and Bitfinex paid $18.5 million to settle with New York, without admitting wrongdoing, and agreed to publish reserve reports and stop serving New Yorkers.

In October 2021 the US derivatives regulator (CFTC) fined Tether $41 million. It found that over 26 months in 2016–2018, Tether held enough dollars to back every USDT on only 27.6% of the days.

Worth knowing

27.6% of 26 months means: in more than two years, the vault was full for only about seven months in total.

Tether's answer.

Tether did not admit wrongdoing and says it has always paid customers who asked for their dollars. Since 2021 it publishes quarterly reserve reports checked by an accounting firm — reports that look at a single day, not a whole year.

2026: the first full audit.

In August 2026 Tether announced that KPMG had completed a full audit of its 2025 accounts with a "clean" opinion — the first in the company's history, about nine years after an audit was first promised.

It does not change what happened in 2016–2018, but it answers the oldest criticism for the most recent year.

Worth knowing

First promised: 2017. Delivered: 2026. Nine years in which the world's biggest stablecoin had no full audit.

In short

Regulators found that in its early years USDT was not always fully backed as claimed. Tether paid fines without admitting wrongdoing, slowly became more open, and completed its first full audit only in 2026.

07Part 03 · Twelve years

6 · May 2022: $7 billion out in 48 hours

QWhat happens when everyone wants their dollar back at once?

Another "dollar" collapses.

TerraUSD (UST) was a digital dollar without a vault of real dollars: its price relied on a formula and on its sister coin, LUNA. In May 2022 the formula broke and UST fell to about 30 cents within days.

Panic spread to every stablecoin.

12 May: USDT dips to 95 cents.

For a few hours USDT traded as low as $0.95. People rushed to swap it for other coins, or to ask Tether for their dollars.

Tether pays — billions.

Tether kept paying $1 for every token. It said it redeemed about $7 billion in 48 hours, and over the following weeks the USDT in circulation fell from about $84 billion to about $73 billion.

Every token handed back was burned.

The anchor holds. A bank did not.

Within days USDT was back near $1. Compare that with Silicon Valley Bank in March 2023: customers tried to pull $42 billion — a quarter of its deposits — in one day, and the bank collapsed the next morning.

SVB had locked its money in long-term bonds it could only sell at a loss. Tether's vault held far more short-term assets that could be turned into cash quickly.

Worth knowing

USDT survived a run of about 9% of its supply in two days; SVB died from a run of 25% in one. What matters is not how big the vault is, but how fast it can turn into cash.

In short

When panic hit in May 2022, USDT briefly fell to $0.95, but Tether paid out billions at $1 and the price recovered. Having real assets in the vault made the difference with TerraUSD.

08Part 03 · Twelve years

7 · From 73 to 184 billion

QWho uses USDT, and where does it travel?

Ten years of growth in one line.

The line shows how many USDT existed. Almost nothing until 2017, a boom in 2021, the 2022 dip — then steady growth.

Supply: Coin Metrics (all chains it tracks) to Dec 2025; dashed: Tether's quarterly reports and DefiLlama.

2023–2025: a record every year.

USDT grew by about $50 billion in 2025 alone, to $186.5 billion at the end of the year. Tether says about 530 million people use it — especially in countries where banks are slow or the local money loses value.

Where USDT lives.

USDT exists on several blockchains. Most of it sits on Tron — cheap and fast to send — and on Ethereum. On 1 October 2026: about $92 billion on Tron, $74 billion on Ethereum, and smaller amounts on BNB Chain and Solana.

Worth knowing

Tron holds about half of all USDT, yet it carried roughly 73% of all stablecoin transfers in Q3 2026: it is where digital dollars actually move, especially in emerging markets.

A dollar that can be frozen.

Unlike cash, Tether can freeze USDT in any wallet. It does so at the request of police and regulators: it says it has frozen more than $4.4 billion linked to scams, hacks and sanctions, working with over 340 agencies in 65 countries.

Worth knowing

Once blacklisted, a wallet almost never comes back: of the addresses Tether blocked in 2025, only about 4 in 100 were ever released.

A new home: El Salvador.

In January 2025 Tether moved its headquarters from the British Virgin Islands to El Salvador, after receiving a licence there.

In short

USDT has become the world's most used digital dollar, travelling mostly on Tron and Ethereum. Unlike cash, it can be frozen by the company that issues it.

09Part 04 · USDT today

8 · USDT in Vietnam: $220 billion a year

QWhy does USDT often cost more than a dollar at the bank?

One of the busiest crypto countries.

In Chainalysis' 2025 ranking of how widely people use crypto, Vietnam came 4th in the world. In the 12 months to June 2025, about $220 billion of crypto flowed to Vietnamese users — and for most of them, USDT is the door in.

Worth knowing

$220 billion of crypto in a year is equal to about 43% of Vietnam's GDP in 2025 ($514 billion). It is a flow, not wealth — but it shows how much money already moves outside the banks.

How Lan buys: P2P.

Big international exchanges have almost no direct trading between the dong and coins. So Lan uses "P2P": on the exchange she picks a seller, transfers dong from her bank account, and the exchange releases the seller's USDT to her. Selling works the other way round.

Why USDT often costs more than a bank dollar.

Buying dollars at a bank to invest is not simple, while demand for USDT is high — so sellers can ask a little more. In late August 2025 P2P USDT reached about 26,900 dong, while the bank rate was around 26,385 — about 2% higher.

The gap moves: in September 2025, after new rules were announced, it shrank and USDT briefly traded below the official rate.

Worth knowing

The premium works like a thermometer: when it is high, Vietnamese demand for dollars and crypto is running hot; when it turns negative, money is leaving crypto.

The risks of P2P.

Some sellers have had bank accounts frozen after unknowingly receiving money from scam victims; some buyers have paid and waited for coins. Experts advise trading only inside the exchange, accepting money only from the buyer's own account, and keeping every record.

New rules are coming.

Resolution 05 (9 September 2025) started a five-year pilot for licensed crypto exchanges. The Ministry of Finance began accepting applications on 20 January 2026, and several companies have passed the first round.

As of mid-November 2026 no exchange had been licensed yet. Six months after the first licence, buying and selling crypto outside licensed exchanges will be penalised.

In short

In Vietnam USDT is the main bridge between the dong and crypto, usually bought P2P at a small premium over the bank dollar. P2P carries real risks, and licensed local exchanges are on the way.

Two roads: America's GENIUS Act demands licensed issuers, full reserves and audits, so Tether launches a separate coin, USAT; Europe's MiCA rules push USDT off regulated exchanges; S&P's gauge points to 5, "weak".

10Part 04 · USDT today

9 · New rules for digital dollars

QIs USDT allowed in the US and Europe?

America: the GENIUS Act.

On 18 July 2025 the US signed its first federal law for stablecoins: issuers serving Americans need a licence, full reserves of safe assets and regular reports.

USDT, run from El Salvador, is not built for those rules — so in January 2026 Tether launched a separate coin for the US, USAT, issued by Anchorage Digital Bank and led by Bo Hines, a former White House crypto adviser.

Worth knowing

A countdown has started: from 18 July 2028, three years after the law was signed, US platforms may only offer approved stablecoins. Unless Tether gets USDT approved, it has less than two years left on American exchanges.

Europe: off the shelves.

The EU's MiCA rules allow only authorised stablecoins on regulated exchanges. Tether chose not to apply. Exchanges removed USDT for European users in 2024–2025, and the last transition period ended on 1 July 2026; Circle's USDC took its place.

A "weak" grade.

In November 2025 the rating agency S&P cut its score for USDT's ability to hold $1 to 5 — "weak", the lowest on its scale — citing more risky assets in the vault and gaps in what Tether discloses. Tether said it strongly disagrees.

11Part 05 · Strengths & weaknesses

What USDT does well

QWhy do hundreds of millions of people use it?

1 · It stays at one dollar.

In normal months USDT trades within a fraction of a cent of $1 — in November 2025 its average closing price was about $0.9997. Its worst slip in years was to $0.95, for a few hours in May 2022.

2 · The biggest and the most used.

With about $184 billion in circulation, USDT is the third-largest crypto asset and the most traded: in November 2025 alone, $3.84 trillion of it changed hands.

3 · Fast, cheap, always open.

USDT crosses borders at any hour, usually in minutes, without a bank in the middle. That is why it is the dollar of choice in countries where banks are slow or money loses value.

4 · It paid when it mattered.

In May 2022 Tether paid out about $7 billion in 48 hours at $1 per token, while TerraUSD — with no real vault — collapsed.

5 · More open than before.

Quarterly reserve reports since 2021, a cushion of about $8 billion, and in 2026 a first full audit by KPMG with a clean opinion.

Five warning cards: a record of broken promises; riskier assets and a thin cushion; one company can freeze it; holders get no interest or protection; outside the rules in the US and Europe.

12Part 05 · Strengths & weaknesses

What can go wrong

QWhat risks come with holding USDT?

1 · A record of broken promises.

US regulators found that USDT was fully backed on only 27.6% of days in 2016–2018. Tether paid $18.5 million and $41 million to settle, without admitting wrongdoing.

2 · Riskier assets, thin cushion.

About 30% of the vault is gold, bitcoin, loans and other investments, above a cushion of about 4%. S&P rates USDT's ability to stay at $1 as "weak" — 5 out of 5.

3 · One company can freeze it.

Tether can block any wallet. It has frozen more than $4.4 billion, mostly linked to crime — but in 2025 only 3.6% of blacklisted addresses were later released, and innocent users can be caught.

4 · No interest, no insurance.

Holders earn nothing while Tether keeps billions in interest. And unlike a bank deposit, USDT has no deposit insurance if something goes wrong.

5 · Outside the rules in the US and Europe.

USDT is not authorised under America's GENIUS Act or Europe's MiCA and has been removed from regulated European exchanges. In Vietnam, buying it P2P carries scam and frozen-account risks.

13Part 06 · Conclusion

Conclusion

QSo, should Lan hold USDT?

USDT does one job well: it is a dollar that moves like crypto.

It has stayed close to $1 for most of twelve years, survived a run in 2022 and is used by hundreds of millions of people. Behind it sits a huge vault, mostly US government debt.

But it is a promise from one private company.

Tether decides what goes into the vault, can freeze any wallet, keeps all the interest, and spent years with a poor record on transparency — only now improving. Outside the US and Europe, few rules protect holders.

Worth knowing

The real question is not "is USDT safe?" but "whose risk am I taking?": a private company in El Salvador that keeps the interest, instead of a bank with deposit insurance.

For Lan: a tool, not a savings account.

To send dollars or park them for a short time, USDT is practical. For large or long-term savings, spread the risk, don't keep everything on one exchange, and in Vietnam move to licensed platforms as they open. This is education, not investment advice.
