SOL — The Ship of Light

Every payment is a beam of light. What Solana is, why it is fast, what can go wrong — in plain words.

What is Solana, and why do people say it's fast?

Solana is a shared record book.

It records who sent money to whom. The book is not kept by a bank: about 650 computers around the world each keep an identical copy.

Nobody can change it alone.

Every new line must be checked and accepted by most of those computers. If one computer edits its own copy, the others refuse it.

SOL is the network's own coin.

Fees are paid in SOL. People also use Solana to send digital dollars (such as USDC), trade tokens and use apps.

Chapter 02 · What it is

20 USDC in under a second

What does a payment on Solana look like?

Nhi sells earrings online.

A customer in Singapore wants to pay 20 USDC — a digital dollar. Nhi sends her wallet address (a QR code), and the customer taps “send”.

Under a second, for less than a cent.

The money arrives almost immediately. The fee is 0.000005 SOL — about $0.0004 at a SOL price of $73.

Worth knowing

Since a big upgrade in October 2026, a payment can no longer be reversed after about 0.15 seconds (the target), down from about 12.8 seconds.

Much faster than other networks.

Each network adds a new page (a “block”) to its book regularly: Bitcoin about every 10 minutes, Ethereum every 12 seconds, Solana every 0.2 seconds.

In short

Three things make Solana fast: turns to write are planned in advance, many payments are processed at once, and a busy spot doesn't make everything expensive. The next three chapters show them.

Four computers in a row; a glowing frame moves from one to the next. A strip of numbered pages shows four pages per computer. A phone sends a beam straight to the computer writing now.

Chapter 03 · Why it's fast

Computers take turns to write

Who writes the new pages?

One computer writes at a time.

At any moment one computer has the pen. It collects payments, writes them on a new page and sends the page to the others to check.

The rota is set for a whole day.

Who writes when is worked out about 24 hours in advance. Each computer writes 4 pages in a row — about 0.8 seconds — then the next one takes over.

🔤 Technical name: leader schedule.

Payments go straight to the writer.

Because everyone knows who writes next, the customer's wallet sends the payment straight to that computer. It doesn't sit in a shared waiting line, as on Bitcoin or Ethereum.

Four payment cards, each naming the wallets it uses. They move along four lanes at once. Two purchases of the same meme coin have to go one after the other in the same lane.

Chapter 04 · Why it's fast

Many payments at the same time

A page holds thousands of payments. How are they all done in time?

Each payment says which wallets it uses.

For example: customer → Nhi; An → Bình. These two payments have nothing in common.

Nothing in common? Do them together.

A computer has many processor cores. Solana gives unrelated payments to different cores and runs them at the same time. Ethereum runs them one by one.

🔤 Technical name: Sealevel.

Same place? One after the other.

When many people buy the same meme coin, those purchases change the same record, so they must wait for each other. That spot slows down; everything else keeps moving.

A tiny fee coin splits: half disappears, half goes to the computer that wrote the page. Then a page bar where one meme coin can take only a small slice.

Chapter 05 · Why it's fast

Cheap fees, even when it's busy

Who gets the fee, and what happens when everyone rushes in?

The basic fee is tiny.

A payment usually pays 0.000005 SOL. Half is destroyed for good; the other half goes to the computer that wrote the page.

In a hurry? Pay a little extra.

When the network is busy, someone in a hurry can add a priority fee to be written first.

One busy spot can't fill the page.

A coin everyone is rushing to buy can use at most about 12% of each page. Those buyers bid up fees among themselves, while Nhi's payment stays cheap.

Chapter 06 · The downside

The network has stopped before

Does going this fast have a downside?

Flooded by bots.

In 2021–2022 automated programs (bots) sent too many transactions at once and the network stopped — once for 17 hours. While it was stopped, nobody could send or receive money.

Then software bugs.

In February 2023 it stopped for almost 19 hours, in February 2024 for about 5 hours. Each time the people running the computers had to restart together.

1,000 days without a stop.

There has been no stop since 6 February 2024. Flooding the network now costs fees, so the jams of the early years are much rarer.

In short

Speed has its own risks. Solana is much steadier now, but its record of stopping is worth knowing.

Chapter 07 · The downside

Fast needs expensive machines

Who can help keep the record book?

Each computer must be very powerful.

A computer keeping Solana's book in 2026 needs 384–512 GB of memory and a 10 Gbps internet line. A typical laptop has 8–16 GB of memory.

So fewer people can do it.

The number of computers fell from 2,560 (March 2023) to about 650 (October 2026), mostly because of the cost.

Fewer computers, more concentrated power.

In October 2026 just 18 of the largest operators together had enough weight to stop the network. Most computers also run the same family of software, so one bug can hit almost all of them.

Chapter 08 · The SOL coin

What SOL is for, and its price

If the network works well, does SOL go up?

Fees and staking.

SOL pays the fees. People can also stake SOL: lock it up to back one of the computers and earn a reward. About 68% of all SOL is staked (May 2026).

New SOL every year.

About 3.6% new SOL is created each year — about 22.9 million coins — and paid as rewards. The rate falls every year; in August 2026 the network voted to make it fall twice as fast.

About 75% below its top.

On 17 November 2026 SOL was about $72.83, against a top of about $293 (January 2025). A busy network does not mean the price will rise.

In short

SOL is really used, but new coins keep being added and the price follows market mood more than network speed.

Chapter 09 · In short

The ship of light: strengths and weaknesses

So what does Solana do well, and what should you watch?

Strengths.

Payments in under a second · fees under one cent · many payments at the same time · a busy spot doesn't raise everyone's fees · 1,000+ days without stopping.

Weaknesses.

Stopped several times in 2021–2024 · expensive machines, so only about 650 computers · power held by few · about 3.6% new SOL a year · a very volatile price.

What it means for Nhi.

Nhi gets 20 USDC almost instantly for almost nothing — that's what Solana does well. Buying SOL is a bet on the coin's price, not just the technology. As of November 2026 Vietnam has no licensed crypto exchange. This is education, not investment advice.
