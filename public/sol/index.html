01Act 01 · Intro

Solana, explained

If crypto is digital money, why couldn't you buy a coffee with it?

No crypto knowledge needed. We start with a coffee.

Minh is a student in Hanoi. He wants to pay a friend back $2 for a coffee — using crypto. Scroll down; the picture tells the story.

On Bitcoin the coffee goes cold. On Ethereum it costs more than the coffee.

Bitcoin confirms a payment roughly every 10 minutes. Ethereum is quicker, but when it is busy the fee can be many times the price of the coffee.

Solana was built to do the opposite: confirm in under a second, for less than a cent.

Five moments made Solana famous.

Speed and low fees, an NFT boom, a near-death experience with FTX, a surprising comeback, and a meme-coin frenzy. We go through them one by one — then weigh the strengths against the weaknesses.

In short

Older blockchains are safe but slow, and can get expensive. Solana's promise is simple: one network fast and cheap enough for everyday use.

02Act 02 · Five milestones

1 · Taking on Ethereum with speed and cost

QWhat did Solana do differently?

2020: an engineer who wanted a blockchain as fast as a phone network.

Anatoly Yakovenko spent more than a decade at Qualcomm, the mobile-chip company. In 2017 he had an idea for making blockchains much faster. Solana started running on 16 March 2020.

Trick 1: a clock everyone trusts.

Most blockchains spend a lot of time with computers asking each other "which payment came first?". Solana added a shared clock that stamps every transaction with its place in line, so nobody needs to ask. Its name was Proof of History.

(In October 2026 a newer, even faster voting system replaced it.)

Trick 2: many checkouts at once.

Like a supermarket with many tills, Solana handles payments that don't affect each other at the same time instead of one by one. People who hold SOL back the computers that run the network (that part is called Proof of Stake).

The result: fast and very cheap.

A simple payment on Solana costs a fraction of a cent and is confirmed in under a second. During busy times on Ethereum, ordinary transactions could cost $50–100.

The price of that speed: running Solana needs powerful, expensive computers — remember this for later.

Ethereum fee example: The Motley Fool, May 2022. Solana fee: 0.000005 SOL per signature (Syndica).

In short

Solana's founders borrowed ideas from mobile networks: a shared clock so computers agree quickly, and many lanes working at the same time. The result: payments in under a second for less than a cent.

03Act 02 · Five milestones

2 · NFTs bring Solana to the crowd

QWhy did so many people suddenly hear about Solana in 2021?

2021: NFTs boom — and Ethereum is too expensive.

NFTs are digital pictures or items with a certificate of ownership on a blockchain. On Ethereum, buying one could cost more in fees than the picture itself. On Solana, the fee was a fraction of a cent.

Magic Eden: Solana's market square.

Launched in September 2021, Magic Eden became the main place to trade Solana NFTs, handling about 90% of that trading within months. Collections like DeGods and y00ts became famous.

"Solana summer".

Newcomers poured in. SOL went from under $2 at the start of 2021 to about $259 in November — more than 100 times higher.

One weekend, two kinds of failure.

On 30 April – 1 May 2022, a huge NFT sale on Ethereum pushed fees into the thousands of dollars per transaction. The same weekend, bots rushing to grab NFTs on Solana flooded it until the network stopped for seven hours.

Lesson: Ethereum gets expensive when busy; Solana stays cheap — but cheap also makes spam easy.

Vice; The Next Web; Solana Foundation outage report.

In short

When buying NFTs on Ethereum got too expensive, collectors moved to Solana and its marketplace Magic Eden. It made Solana famous — and showed its weak spot: cheap transactions also invite floods of bots.

04Act 02 · Five milestones

3 · FTX collapses — Solana seems dead

QWhat happened when Solana's biggest backer went bankrupt?

FTX: Solana's biggest friend.

Sam Bankman-Fried's exchange FTX and trading firm Alameda invested early, built apps on Solana and promoted it everywhere. They held a huge pile of SOL — in court, these coins were called "Sam coins".

November 2022: FTX goes bankrupt.

FTX had used its customers' money. Investors feared all that SOL would be dumped. SOL fell from about $35 to $13 in days, and to around $8 by the end of December — down 97% from its peak.

"Solana is dead."

Headlines said it; many believed it. On Christmas Day 2022, DeGods and y00ts — Solana's two most popular NFT collections — announced they would move to Ethereum and Polygon.

But the network never stopped.

Solana itself was not FTX: blocks kept coming, and many developers stayed. Ironically, when the bankrupt FTX later sold its SOL in 2024, buyers paid $64 and $102 per coin — far above the bottom.

In short

FTX and its sister firm Alameda were Solana's loudest backers and held lots of SOL. When FTX collapsed in November 2022, SOL fell about 97% from its peak and many called Solana dead. But the network itself kept running.

SOL's price through 2023 rises from about $10 to over $100; gift boxes open for BONK at Christmas 2022, then Pyth and Jito in late 2023; a large label shows +900%.

05Act 02 · Five milestones

4 · A spectacular comeback

QHow did Solana come back from the dead?

Christmas 2022: a gift called BONK.

A dog-themed meme coin, BONK, gave half of all its tokens away for free to about 297,000 Solana wallets — NFT holders, artists and developers. No investors, just a gift. It brought the fun back.

2023: quietly rebuilding.

For most of the year SOL sat around $20. The headlines moved on, but many teams kept building apps on Solana.

"Airdrop" season: free tokens for early users.

An airdrop is when a project gives its token away to early users. In November 2023 Pyth handed out tokens worth about $87 million; in December Jito gave away about $225 million. People rushed to try Solana apps, hoping to be next.

More than 900% in one year.

SOL started 2023 at about $10 and ended above $100. In just over a year, Solana went from "dead" to one of the hottest blockchains again.

In short

A playful community gift (BONK), builders who stayed, and a season of free-token giveaways brought users back. SOL rose more than 900% in 2023 — one of the biggest comebacks in crypto.

06Act 02 · Five milestones

5 · Meme coins and Pump.fun

QWhy did Solana become the home of meme coins — and who lost?

Pump.fun: a new coin in seconds, for cents.

Launched in January 2024, Pump.fun lets anyone create a token with just a name and a picture. Early buyers pay less; if enough people buy, the token moves to a normal exchange.

95,578 new tokens in one day.

New tokens on Solana peaked on 26 January 2025 — Pump.fun made about 60% of them. Almost all were worth close to nothing within days, but the trading made Solana very busy.

TRUMP and LIBRA.

In January 2025 Donald Trump launched the TRUMP meme coin on Solana; its value briefly approached $15 billion before falling more than 80%. Within three weeks, about 813,000 wallets had lost a combined $2 billion. In February, LIBRA — promoted by Argentina's president — lost about $4.4 billion of value within hours.

The lawsuit: "a digital casino" (alleged).

A U.S. class action claims Pump.fun ran an unlicensed casino that took more than $5.5 billion from users, and names Solana Labs, the Solana Foundation and their leaders. They deny it. The case is still going on.

Status: allegations in a civil case, not court findings.

"Memecoins do not define Solana."

That is how Solana Foundation president Lily Liu put it in May 2026. The foundation now pushes payments and digital dollars instead. The meme frenzy has cooled.

In short

Cheap, fast transactions made Solana the main place for meme coins. A few people got rich; most buyers lost. A U.S. lawsuit calls it a rigged casino — Pump.fun and the Solana organisations deny it, and no court has ruled.

08Act 03 · Solana today

Solana today

QWhat do people actually use Solana for in 2026?

Swapping right inside your wallet.

Minh wants to swap SOL for USDC, a digital dollar. He uses Jupiter, which finds the best price across exchanges such as Raydium. No bank account, no paperwork — just his wallet.

Digital dollars pour in.

Digital dollars (stablecoins) on Solana reached a record $17.5 billion in September 2026. Western Union chose Solana for its own digital dollar, and Samsung Wallet supports USDC on Solana.

Wall Street steps in.

Since late 2025, ordinary investors can buy SOL through funds on the stock exchange. Tokenised shares and funds on Solana reached $5 billion, and the DTCC — which settles most U.S. stock trades — plans to connect to Solana in 2027.

More reliable — but the price cooled.

Solana has run 1,000 days in a row without stopping, and an October 2026 upgrade makes payments final in about 0.15 seconds. Still, SOL trades near $70–75, more than 40% below a year earlier.

In short

Beyond memes, Solana is now used for swapping, holding digital dollars and even tokenised shares. Big names — PayPal, Western Union, the DTCC — build on it. But most activity is still trading.

09Act 03 · Solana today

The people behind it

QWho steers Solana today?

Three faces, a few organisations.

Anatoly Yakovenko and Raj Gokal co-founded Solana Labs, the company that built the network. Lily Liu leads the Solana Foundation, a non-profit in Switzerland that funds and promotes the ecosystem. All three are named in the Pump.fun lawsuit and deny the claims.

Who writes the software?

Most of the core engineers now work at Anza, which maintains the main software. Jump Crypto wrote a second, independent version (Firedancer), so one bug can no longer stop everything.

In short

No single company controls Solana, but a handful of organisations write most of its software and steer its direction.

10Act 03 · Solana today

Bitcoin vs Ethereum vs Solana

QHow does Solana compare with the two biggest coins?

Three designs, three trade-offs.

Bitcoin stays simple and slow so almost anyone can check it. Ethereum stays careful and sends busy traffic to cheaper side networks. Solana does everything on one fast network — which needs bigger, pricier machines.

The table beside the picture lines them up.

In short

Bitcoin is the slow, scarce one; Ethereum the big app platform; Solana the fast, cheap one. Solana is much smaller by value — about $43 billion.

11Act 04 · Strengths & weaknesses

Strengths

QWhat does Solana do well?

1. Fast.

A payment is confirmed in under a second — and since October 2026 it becomes final in about 0.15 seconds.

2. Cheap.

A simple transfer costs a fraction of a cent, so small payments, games and apps make sense.

3. Really used.

About 100 million user transactions a day. In August 2026 Solana handled more than all other major blockchains combined.

crypto.news; Solana Compass.

4. Big money has arrived.

$17.5 billion in digital dollars, U.S. stock-market funds holding SOL, and companies like PayPal, Western Union and the DTCC building on it.

5. Resilient.

It survived the FTX collapse, has run 1,000 days without stopping, and now has two independent versions of its software.

In short

Fast, cheap, heavily used, trusted by big companies, and tough enough to survive FTX.

Five warning cards light up one at a time: outages and hacks, power in few hands, the meme-coin reputation, wild price swings, and new coins printed every year.

12Act 04 · Strengths & weaknesses

Weaknesses

QWhat should worry you about Solana?

1. It has stopped — and been hacked.

Between 2021 and 2024 the network fully stopped several times, once for 17 hours and once for about 19. In 2022 a hacker took about $320 million from Wormhole, a bridge connected to Solana.

2. Power in few hands.

Fewer than 800 computers run the network — down about two-thirds since 2023 — and they are expensive. Early investors, the team and the foundation got most of the first coins, and a few organisations write most of the software.

3. A meme-coin reputation.

Pump.fun, TRUMP and LIBRA made Solana look like a casino. Most meme buyers lost money, and a lawsuit (still only allegations) names Solana's leaders.

4. Wild price swings.

SOL fell 97% from 2021 to 2022, and in November 2026 it is about 75% below its January 2025 record. Its price depends heavily on how much people trade.

5. New coins printed every year.

There is no maximum number of SOL. About 3.8% new SOL is created each year as rewards; holders who don't take part slowly own a smaller share. (A vote in 2026 decided to cut this faster.)

In short

Past outages, power concentrated in a few hands, a meme-coin casino reputation, extreme price swings and no supply cap.

13Act 04 · Strengths & weaknesses

Weighing it up

QSo which side is heavier?

Strengths on the left…

Fast, cheap, really used, big money, resilient.

…weaknesses on the right.

Outages and hacks, few hands, meme reputation, wild swings, endless supply. The scale ends up close to level — which is exactly why people argue about Solana.

The last twelve months.

Usage hit records while the price fell by more than 40%. The network and the coin's price no longer move together.

In short

The technology has clearly improved; the price has not followed. Both sides of the scale are real.

14Act 05 · Conclusion

Conclusion

QSo — what is Solana, after six years?

Conclusion: fast, tough — and still risky.

Solana did what it promised: Minh's $2 arrives in under a second for less than a cent. It also survived a crash that would have killed most projects, and won over big companies.

The price of that speed is real: fewer, more powerful operators, a history of outages, and a reputation tied to meme coins.

What this means for you.

As a user: Solana is a convenient, cheap way to send money and swap tokens — but stay away from meme coins you don't understand.

As an investor: SOL has fallen 75–97% more than once. A strong network does not guarantee a rising price. Only risk money you can afford to lose. This is not investment advice.

In short

Solana proved a blockchain can be as fast and cheap as a payment app, and tough enough to survive FTX. In exchange it accepts fewer, more powerful operators, an outage history and a speculative crowd. A great network and a safe coin are two different questions.
