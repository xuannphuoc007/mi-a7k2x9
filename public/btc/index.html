September 2008: trust breaks.

Banks fail and governments rescue them with public money. Some people start asking: what if money didn't depend on trusting banks at all?

January 2009: block 0.

Satoshi Nakamoto — still anonymous today — switches the network on. The very first block carries a newspaper headline about bank bailouts: a note about why Bitcoin exists.

In short

Bitcoin was born out of the 2008 crisis: money that runs on code instead of trust in banks.

02Chapter 02

Two pizzas, 10,000 BTC

Worth nothing — at first.

For more than a year bitcoin had no price at all. A few hobbyists mined it on home computers and sent it to each other for fun.

The most expensive pizzas ever.

On 22 May 2010, Laszlo Hanyecz paid 10,000 BTC for two pizzas. Fans now celebrate the date every year as Bitcoin Pizza Day.

A price appears.

Exchanges open in mid-2010, with prices under 10 cents. By February 2011, one bitcoin is worth one dollar.

In short

A price only exists once people start trading. Bitcoin went from $0 to $1 in about two years.

03Chapter 03

21 million, forever

The rules are written in the code.

There will only ever be 21 million bitcoin. New coins appear as a reward for adding a block to the chain, about every 10 minutes.

Every four years: the halving.

Every 210,000 blocks — roughly four years — the reward is cut in half: 50 → 25 → 12.5 → 6.25 → 3.125 BTC. Changing this would need almost everyone running Bitcoin to agree.

Scarcity by design.

More than 95% of all bitcoin already exists. The last tiny fraction will trickle out until around the year 2140.

In short

Fixed supply, shrinking issuance — a schedule anyone can check.

04Chapter 04

Boom, bust, Mt. Gox

The first bubbles.

In 2011 the price jumps from $1 to $29 — then crashes. In 2013 it rockets past $1,000 — then crashes again.

Mt. Gox: when a company fails.

Most trading ran through one exchange in Tokyo. In 2014 it collapsed and about 850,000 BTC went missing. The Bitcoin network kept running, but people who had left coins with Mt. Gox lost them.

Boom and bust is the pattern.

Every big rise so far has been followed by a fall of roughly 75–90%. So far the price later went higher — but the past is no promise about the future.

In short

A company can fail even when Bitcoin doesn't. Wild swings are part of the story.

05Chapter 05

Proof of work

Who keeps the record honest?

Miners. They race to solve a puzzle by guessing numbers over and over. The first to find an answer adds the next block and earns the reward.

Try it yourself.

The puzzle: find a number that makes the block's fingerprint (its 'hash') start with enough zeros. There's no shortcut — you can only keep guessing.

From laptops to warehouses.

In 2009 a home computer was enough. By 2026 the network makes about a billion trillion guesses every second. That enormous cost is what makes cheating impractical.

In short

Security comes from real machines and real energy: rewriting history would cost more than it's worth.

06Chapter 06

Mania and winter

2017: everyone hears about Bitcoin.

The price climbs from about $1,000 to nearly $20,000 in one year. People who had never heard of it ask how to buy.

A war over block size.

For years developers and miners argued about how many transactions should fit in a block. In August 2017 a group split off to create Bitcoin Cash. Tap the events to explore.

Then: winter.

By December 2018 the price is down about 84%. Many projects born in the boom disappear.

In short

Hype brings people in — and the crash shakes them out.

07Chapter 07

Crash, then Wall Street

March 2020: everything crashes.

As COVID spreads, investors sell everything at once. Bitcoin drops nearly 40% in a single day.

Then the companies arrive.

Central banks pump money into the economy. Companies like MicroStrategy and Tesla buy bitcoin, Coinbase lists on Nasdaq, El Salvador makes it legal money — and when China pushes miners out, the network keeps going.

A new peak.

In November 2021, bitcoin closes near $67,500.

In short

Bitcoin went from internet money to a boardroom topic.

08Chapter 08

The year of falling dominoes

When lenders lend to lenders.

Many crypto companies had lent to each other, often with borrowed money. When one fell, it knocked over the next.

Push the first domino.

Watch Terra, Three Arrows Capital, Celsius, Voyager, FTX and BlockFi fall one after another.

The network didn't stop.

Every company in the chain was a middleman. Bitcoin kept producing blocks every ten minutes — but people who trusted those companies with their coins lost money.

In short

‘Not your keys, not your coins’: much of the risk sits with the companies around Bitcoin.

09Chapter 09

ETFs, $100k and a reserve

January 2024: Wall Street opens the door.

US regulators approve spot Bitcoin ETFs. Pension funds and financial advisors can now buy bitcoin through ordinary accounts.

Halving, $100k, a national reserve.

The fourth halving arrives in April 2024, $100,000 in December 2024, and in March 2025 a US order to keep seized bitcoin as a strategic reserve.

A record — then a record crash.

Bitcoin reaches about $126,000 on 6 October 2025. Four days later a tariff shock wipes out $19 billion of leveraged bets in a day.

In short

Bitcoin is now tied to big finance — and to its moods.

10Chapter 10

The hangover — and your turn

2026: the hangover.

From its record, bitcoin falls to about $60,000 in February 2026 as borrowed money is unwound.

Down, up, hacked.

June is the worst month since 2022. August is the best August since 2017. Then the Fed raises rates, and on 29 October hackers steal $6.3 billion from Binance.

Where we are now.

In mid-November 2026 bitcoin trades in the low-to-mid $60,000s — about half its record, yet roughly 700,000 times its first market price. The story isn't finished. Explore all of it below.
