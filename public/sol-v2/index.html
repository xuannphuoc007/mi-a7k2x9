Solana · explained simply

SOL — The Ship of Light

Every transaction is a beam of light. This is how Solana carries more than a thousand of them a second — and what that costs.

How can Solana be this fast and this cheap?

Nhi gets paid.

Nhi sells handmade earrings online. A customer in Singapore pays her 20 USDC (a digital dollar) from a Solana wallet.

Final in under a second, for almost nothing.

The basic fee is 0.000005 SOL per signature — about $0.0004 at a SOL price of $73. Since October 2026 the payment is usually final in under a second.

Three networks, three speeds.

Bitcoin adds a block about every 10 minutes, Ethereum every 12 seconds, Solana every 0.2 seconds.

In short

Solana is not fast because of one trick but because of several design choices working together. The next six chapters take them one at a time.

Chapter 02 · Why it's fast

Everyone knows who is next

Who puts transactions into a block?

Validators take turns.

About 650 computers (validators) run Solana. In each turn one of them is the leader: it collects transactions, packs them into blocks and sends the blocks to the others to check.

🔤 Real name: leader.

The rota is set for a whole day.

Who leads when is worked out in advance for an epoch of 432,000 blocks — about 24 hours at today's speed. A validator with more SOL staked on it gets more turns.

🔤 Real name: leader schedule.

Four blocks each, then the next one.

Each leader keeps four blocks in a row — 0.8 seconds — then the next leader on the list takes over. Nobody waits for a vote on who goes next.

Left: a waiting room crowded with transactions. Right: a Solana wallet sends a beam straight to the current leader and the next two leaders.

Chapter 03 · Why it's fast

Sent straight to the leader

Where does a transaction go before it is in a block?

Bitcoin and Ethereum have a waiting room.

New transactions are passed between computers and sit in a shared queue (the mempool) until a block producer picks them.

Solana sends them straight.

Because the rota is known, the wallet's transaction goes directly to the current leader and the next few leaders. The next leader can get ready before its turn.

🔤 Real name: Gulf Stream.

Transactions expire.

Each transaction names a recent block. If it is not included within 150 blocks — about 30 seconds now — it expires and the wallet sends it again.

A tape of numbers ticks forward. Transactions are stamped at step 1,204 and 1,207. Checkers verify pieces of the tape side by side. Then a badge: Alpenglow, 21 October 2026.

Chapter 04 · Why it's fast

A counter that proves the order

How do computers agree what came first without asking each other?

The leader runs a counter that never stops.

The computer repeats one hash calculation (SHA-256), feeding each result into the next. Each step has to wait for the one before, so the length of the chain shows that time has passed.

🔤 Real name: Proof of History (PoH).

Every transaction gets a step number.

If the customer's payment lands at step 1,204 and another at step 1,207, everyone can see which came first. Checking the chain is much faster than making it, because checkers can split it up and work in parallel.

Since 21 October 2026: a new way to vote.

The Alpenglow upgrade replaced how validators vote to confirm blocks. Agreement no longer depends on this counter, and final confirmation dropped from about 12.8 seconds to a target of 0.15 seconds.

Worth knowing

The counter was Solana's founding idea in 2017. Nine years later the network moved its voting to a faster design.

A block breaks into small pieces plus spare pieces. The leader sends them to a small group, which forwards them to the next layer. One lost piece is rebuilt from the spares.

Chapter 05 · Why it's fast

Cutting a block into small pieces

How do you get a big block to hundreds of computers quickly?

Cut the block into pieces.

The leader cuts each block into small pieces (shreds) and adds spare pieces. If some get lost on the way, the receiver can still rebuild the whole block.

🔤 Real name: shreds with erasure coding.

Pass them on in layers.

The leader does not send to every computer itself. It sends each piece to a small group, which forwards it to the next. If each computer forwards to 200 others, two layers reach 40,000.

🔤 Real name: Turbine.

The lesson of 2023.

In February 2023 one validator sent out an oversized block — about 150,000 pieces instead of a few hundred. The filters were overwhelmed and the network stopped for almost 19 hours.

Four lanes of light. Transactions that touch different accounts run in different lanes at the same time; two that touch the same meme coin queue in one lane.

Chapter 06 · Why it's fast

Running transactions side by side

A block holds thousands of transactions. How do they all run in time?

Each transaction says what it will touch.

A Solana transaction must list the accounts it will read and change. For example: customer → Nhi's wallet; An → Bình's wallet.

🔤 Real name: Sealevel.

No overlap? Run at the same time.

Transactions that don't change the same account are spread over many processor cores and run at once. Ethereum runs its transactions one after another.

Overlap? Queue up.

When many people buy the same meme coin, those transactions all change the same account, so they run one by one. The busy spot slows down; the rest of the network carries on.

A fee coin splits: half burns, half goes to the leader. A priority fee goes fully to the leader. Then a block bar of 100 million units with a hot account capped at 12 million.

Chapter 07 · Why it's fast

Cheap fees — and a jam stays local

Who gets the fee, and what happens when the network is busy?

Basic fee: half is burned.

Each signature pays 0.000005 SOL. Half is burned (destroyed for good); the other half goes to the leader that made the block.

In a hurry? Add a priority fee.

When it's busy, users can add a priority fee to be placed first. Since February 2025 all of this fee goes to the leader; none of it is burned any more.

One hot spot can use at most 12% of a block.

Since 29 July 2026 a block can hold 100 million compute units, but one busy account can use at most 12 million. When a meme coin goes wild, fees rise there — Nhi's payments stay cheap.

🔤 Real name: local fee markets.

In short

Fees stay tiny for most people because a rush on one coin can only use a slice of each block.

Chapter 08 · The price of speed

The times the network stopped (2021–2024)

What is the risk of going this fast?

2021–2022: flooded by bots.

In September 2021 bots flooded a token sale and the network stopped for 17 hours. In April–May 2022 NFT-minting bots stopped it again for about 7 hours.

2023–2024: software bugs.

In February 2023 an oversized block stopped it for almost 19 hours. In February 2024 a bug in the code that loads programs stopped it for about 5 hours. Each time validators had to restart together.

Since then: 1,000 days without a halt.

There has been no full stop since 6 February 2024; the 1,000-day mark came around 2 November 2026. Spam now costs priority fees, so the jams of the early years are much rarer.

In short

Solana has fixed many weak points, but its history of halts is why many institutions still watch it closely.

Chapter 09 · The price of speed

Running a validator is expensive

Who can take part in running the network?

Server-class hardware.

A Solana validator in 2026 needs about 24 or more processor cores, 384–512 GB of memory, enterprise NVMe drives and a 10 Gbps line. A typical laptop has 8–16 GB of memory.

Far fewer validators.

From 2,560 validators in March 2023 to 795 in January 2026. When Alpenglow switched on on 21 October 2026, 654 took part.

Worth knowing

In October 2026, about 18 of the largest operators together held enough stake to halt the network.

Mostly one family of software.

Most validators run Agave or a version built from it. Jump's Firedancer is written from scratch, but it could not join the switch to Alpenglow, so on 21 October 2026 the network moved over on Agave-based software alone.

Worth knowing

A bug in shared software can hit almost the whole network at once.

Three gauges: block capacity 60 → 100 million; block time 400 → 200 ms; final confirmation 12.8 s → 0.15 s target.

Chapter 10 · Faster still

Three speed-ups in 2026

What is Solana changing?

Bigger blocks.

29 July 2026: each block's capacity rose from 60 million to 100 million compute units (+66%).

Blocks twice as often.

From 400 to 200 milliseconds, in four steps; the last one took effect on 9 October 2026. A transaction's expiry window is now 30 seconds.

Final almost at once.

Alpenglow (21 October 2026) targets final confirmation in about 0.15 seconds instead of 12.8. One independent sample of the first 24 hours measured a median of about 0.4 seconds.

Worth knowing

It was the first time Solana changed how it reaches agreement since launch in 2020.

A SOL coin: fees flow in, half burns. New SOL flows out to stakers. An inflation line falls toward 1.5%, sooner under the new plan. A price card: $72.83, 75% below the top.

Chapter 11 · SOL & verdict

SOL: fees, staking and new coins

Does a well-running network mean a higher SOL price?

SOL pays fees and is staked.

About 68% of all SOL is staked on validators (May 2026). Stakers earn from newly issued SOL and from fees.

New SOL every year.

On 18 October 2026 issuance was 3.60% a year on a supply of about 636 million SOL — about 22.9 million new SOL a year, or more than 62,000 a day.

Issuance will shrink twice as fast.

In August 2026 validators voted to cut the issuance rate by 30% a year instead of 15%, reaching the 1.5% floor around 2029 instead of 2032. The change still has to be switched on in the software.

The price: about 75% below its top.

On 17 November 2026 SOL was about $72.83 — down about 44% in a year and about 75% below its top of about $293 (January 2025). U.S. investors have been able to buy SOL funds (ETFs) since October 2025.

Worth knowing

Record network use, weak price: a good network does not automatically lift its token.

In short

SOL is really used, but supply still grows about 3.6% a year and the price follows market mood more than network speed.

Chapter 12 · SOL & verdict

The ship of light: strengths and weaknesses

So what makes Solana fast — and what does it trade away?

Strengths.

A rota known in advance · no waiting room · transactions side by side · basic fee 0.000005 SOL · a jam stays local · final in under a second · 1,000+ days without a halt.

Weaknesses.

Network halts in 2021–2024 · expensive hardware, so few validators (≈650) · mostly one family of software · about 3.6% new SOL a year · a volatile price (−75% from the top).

What it means for Nhi.

Nhi gets 20 USDC almost instantly for a negligible fee — that is what Solana does well. Buying SOL is a bet on the token, not just the technology. As of November 2026 Vietnam has no licensed crypto exchange. This is education, not investment advice.
