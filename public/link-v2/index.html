The Missing Link

How Chainlink carries truth into blockchains — and what that means for the LINK token.

A blockchain cannot see the world. So who carries the truth in — and why trust them?

Meet Khoa.

Khoa builds a small lending app in Saigon: lock ETH, borrow dollars. To know how much to lend, his app needs the price of ETH — right now.

A blockchain is a sealed glass box.

Everything inside runs perfectly, but nothing inside can look out. A smart contract can't open a website, read an exchange or ask a bank. Prices, reserves, random numbers — all of it lives outside the glass.

Someone has to carry facts in.

That messenger is called an oracle. If one messenger lies or goes silent, every app that trusts it pays. Chainlink is built so that no single messenger can do either.

Worth knowing

Chainlink says its oracles had enabled more than $36 trillion of transaction value by Q3 2026.

Glass blocks for Ethereum, Solana, Base, Arbitrum and BNB, all linked by one chain of blue hexagons that belongs to none of them.

Link 02 · The gap

Chainlink has no glass box of its own

Ethereum, Solana and BNB are blockchains. What is Chainlink?

Not a blockchain.

Chainlink runs no blocks and sells no block space. LINK itself is a token that lives on Ethereum (an ERC-677 token: a normal token that can carry an instruction with a payment).

A chain that touches every box.

The same network of independent operators serves dozens of blockchains at once. Each service is run by its own small group of nodes, called a DON.

So it earns differently.

ETH, SOL and BNB earn from people paying to use their chain (gas). LINK earns only when someone pays for a Chainlink service: data, cross-chain transfers, reserve checks.

Worth knowing

This is the root of the big LINK question in chapter 11: the network can be busy while the token stays weak.

In short

Chainlink is not a blockchain. It is the chain between blockchains — and LINK earns from services, not from block space.

Nine hexagon nodes each fetch a price, talk to each other off-chain, agree on the middle value, sign one shared report, and only one transaction enters the glass block.

Link 03 · How the link works

Many nodes, one signature

If many messengers bring a price, which one does Khoa's app believe?

Each node asks its own sources.

Exchanges and data companies quote slightly different prices. Every node collects its own.

They agree outside the chain.

The nodes talk to each other off-chain and take the median — the middle value. A broken or dishonest node at the edge can't drag the result.

They all sign one report.

Instead of nine separate messages, the network produces one report signed by a quorum of nodes. The blockchain checks the signatures.

Only one transaction goes in.

One node delivers the signed report. This design (Off-Chain Reporting, live since February 2021) cut node gas costs by up to 90%.

Worth knowing

Nine messengers, one envelope, many seals: that is why the price in Khoa's app is both cheap and hard to fake.

In short

Many independent nodes, one median, one signed report, one transaction. Cheaper — and no single node decides.

Left: a price pushed into the block when it moves 0.5% or every hour. Right: a fast trade pulls a fresh signed price in under a second and checks the seals itself.

Link 04 · How the link works

Push or pull

Does the price arrive on a schedule, or when you need it?

Push: Data Feeds.

The ETH/USD feed writes a new price when the market moves 0.5% — or after one hour, whichever comes first. Good for lending apps like Khoa's.

Pull: Data Streams.

Fast trading apps can't wait. With Data Streams, signed prices are ready off-chain every moment; the app grabs one inside its own transaction and the chain verifies the signatures on the spot.

Same seals, different delivery.

Both are signed by a decentralized oracle network. Push is simple and always there; pull is faster and you pay only when you use it.

A stablecoin printer linked to a vault meter: printing beyond the reserve is blocked. Then a lottery: a random number arrives with a proof; a tampered number fails the check.

Link 05 · How the link works

Checking vaults, rolling dice

What else can't a blockchain see?

Proof of Reserve: look inside the vault.

A token backed by dollars or bitcoin is only as good as its reserve. Chainlink nodes report the reserve on-chain, so the blockchain can see it.

No reserve, no printing.

The token's own contract can refuse to mint if new supply would exceed the reported reserve. Issuers such as 21Shares use it for their products.

VRF: a fair dice roll.

Blockchains are predictable by design, so they can't roll dice. Chainlink VRF delivers a random number together with a cryptographic proof.

A fake roll gets caught.

If anyone changes the number, the proof no longer matches and the contract rejects it. Games and NFT draws use this to stay fair.

Worth knowing

Same idea every time: bring an outside fact in with evidence the blockchain can check itself.

Two glass blocks joined by a gate. The main network commits a batch; a second network written in a different language checks it and either blesses it or curses the whole gate shut.

Link 06 · How the link works

A gate with two guards

How do you move value between glass boxes without being robbed?

Bridges were the weakest link.

In 2022 Ronin lost $624 million, Wormhole $325 million, Nomad $190 million. Chainalysis counted 64% of DeFi hack losses that year came from bridges.

Guard one commits, guard two checks.

In Chainlink's CCIP (live since July 2023), one network commits a batch of transfers. A separate Risk Management Network re-checks it. The two are written in different languages (Go and Rust) by different teams, so one bug is unlikely to fool both.

Bless — or curse.

Only a blessed batch is executed. If the second guard sees something wrong, it can "curse" the system and pause transfers until it is fixed.

Hacks elsewhere brought traffic here.

After a $292 million exploit at KelpDAO, projects holding over $3 billion moved to CCIP. CCIP 2.0 launched on 28 September 2026 after more than $84 billion of transfers.

In short

CCIP adds a second, independent network that must approve every batch — and can stop everything if something looks wrong.

Link 07 · LINK economics

Staking is an alarm bell

What happens when a messenger goes silent?

A deposit, not a savings account.

Node operators and the community lock LINK in a staking pool of up to 45 million LINK (v0.2, November 2023).

Three hours of silence rings the bell.

If the ETH/USD feed on Ethereum goes more than three hours without a valid update, a staker can raise an alert on-chain.

Penalty and reward.

A valid alert cuts 700 LINK from each node operator serving the feed and pays 7,000 LINK to the alerter. Community stakers are not slashed.

Worth knowing

The penalty is small and covers one feed so far — a start, not a full insurance policy.

Link 08 · LINK economics

Two flows of LINK

Where does LINK come from, and where does it go?

1 billion LINK, fixed since 2017.

35% sold in the 2017 token sale (about $32 million), 35% for node operators and the ecosystem, 30% kept by the company.

Pay with anything, end up as LINK.

Since 2025, users can pay for Chainlink services in other tokens; Payment Abstraction converts the fees into LINK automatically. Since August 2025 part of it goes into the Chainlink Reserve, with no withdrawals planned for years.

The Reserve: 6.68 million LINK.

By 13 November 2026 the Reserve held about 6.68 million LINK (≈ $75 million). It bought about 447,000 LINK in the 30 days to 5 November.

The other pipe is wider.

About 19 million LINK leave the company's non-circulating wallets each quarter, most of it to Binance — roughly 6 million a month.

Worth knowing

≈ 6 million out vs ≈ 0.45 million in each month: about 14 to 1.

In short

Usage now buys LINK — but the company still releases about 14 times more LINK than the Reserve buys.

Link 09 · The journey

The journey so far

How did LINK get here?

2017: whitepaper and sale.

Sergey Nazarov and Steve Ellis, with Ari Juels, raised $32 million selling 350 million LINK at roughly 9–11 cents. For almost two years LINK stayed under $1.30.

2020: DeFi summer.

Lending and trading apps boomed and all needed prices. LINK reached $20.30 in August 2020 while an anonymous "Zeus Capital" called it a scam.

10 May 2021: the top.

LINK hit its all-time high of $52.88 — about 580 times the sale price.

May 2022: a stuck link.

When LUNA collapsed to $0.01, Chainlink's LUNA feed kept reporting a built-in minimum of $0.107. Attackers borrowed against the inflated price; Venus lost about $11.2 million.

Worth knowing

The lesson for apps like Khoa's: the oracle is one link — check its answers and keep a pause button.

Link 10 · The journey

Banks hook into the chain

Why do Swift, UBS and DTCC work with a crypto project?

Swift: 17 banks piloting.

Swift connects over 11,500 institutions. Its blockchain ledger connects banks through the Chainlink platform; 17 banks are piloting it.

UBS: a real transaction.

On 4 November 2025 UBS completed a live subscription and redemption of its tokenised money-market fund using Chainlink's Digital Transfer Agent standard.

CRE: one control room for many links.

The Chainlink Runtime Environment (live since 4 November 2025) lets institutions combine data, reserves, compliance and cross-chain transfers in one workflow. DTCC plans a go-live in Q4 2026.

Worth knowing

Many of these are pilots or planned launches. A big name is not the same as daily volume.

The chain keeps growing. Why doesn't the price?

About $9, far from the top.

LINK closed at $8.85 on 15 November 2026 — about 84% below its 2021 high. Market value is around $7 billion.

Wall Street can buy it.

Grayscale's GLNK became the first US spot Chainlink ETF on 2 December 2025; CME LINK futures started in February 2026. US spot ETFs held about $175 million in November 2026.

Still the default, not the only one.

Link 12 · Today

Strong links, weak links

What should a LINK holder watch?

Strong links.

Default oracle for most big DeFi lending apps since 2019 · no single node decides · a second guard on cross-chain transfers · banks and market infrastructure build with it · fees now buy LINK for the Reserve.

Weak links.

LINK earns from services, not block space, and fees can arrive in other tokens · about 19 million LINK unlocked each quarter · one company steers products and much of the supply · faster rivals in trading · oracles can still be wrong (LUNA, 2022).

Link 13 · Conclusion

The chain is not the token

So what is LINK, in one sentence?

Chainlink is the missing link.

It carries prices, reserves, random numbers and transfers into and between blockchains — with many independent nodes, shared signatures and a second guard.

But the chain is not the token.

Khoa will use Chainlink's feed, with his own safety checks. If you buy LINK, you bet on the token: watch the unlocks, the Reserve and how much revenue is paid in LINK. As of November 2026 Vietnam has no licensed crypto exchange. This is education, not investment advice.
